Florida auto repair market context

Florida is the third-largest state for auto repair SBA lending: 318 loans FY2020-2025 (5.87% national share), $176 million in total approved capital, and $553K average deal size. Deal sizes run 16% above the national auto repair baseline of $477K, with median loan at $268,000 vs. $225,500 nationally (+19%). YoY volume growth is +21.2%, close to the national auto-repair rate of +26.9%.

Metro distribution: Miami, Orlando, Tampa, Jacksonville

Four Florida metros carry the bulk of auto repair SBA volume: Miami / Fort Lauderdale, Orlando, Tampa Bay, and Jacksonville. Florida's population growth combined with high vehicle-count per capita drives strong recurring auto repair demand. Franchise-brand shops (Midas, Meineke, AAMCO, Precision Tune) account for a meaningful share of Florida auto-repair SBA volume alongside independent-shop acquisitions.

Honest framing: charge-off runs 2.08× SBA average

Florida auto repair SBA charges off at 2.83% -- a 2.08× ratio versus the SBA cross-industry average of 1.36% and 2.83× the national auto-repair rate of 1.00%. This is one of the more elevated state-industry combinations in the SBA dataset. Drivers of the elevated performance:

  • Rapid-growth-market instability. Florida's rapid population growth has drawn many operators into shop ownership without established customer bases. Files with less-established books charge off at elevated rates.
  • Hurricane exposure on receivables and facilities. Coastal Florida shops face hurricane-related revenue disruption and facility damage risk, with property-insurance premiums compressing DSCR.
  • Tourism-market volatility. Shops in tourism-heavy metros (Orlando corridor, coastal areas) can see revenue swings between peak and off-season that stress cash flow.
  • Specialty shop concentration. Florida has meaningful volume in specialty categories (transmission specialists, brake shops, mobile mechanics) that carry different risk profiles than general repair.

Individual files with established customer bases, clean AHCA / DBPR licensing (where applicable), disciplined operators, and hurricane-preparedness plans still underwrite favorably. Specialist Florida shop lenders factor these structural risks into projections rather than applying national auto-repair benchmarks that miss Florida's reality.

Florida regulatory context for auto repair SBA underwriting

  • Motor Vehicle Repair Act registration. Florida requires motor vehicle repair shops to register with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS). Registration is straightforward but must be current on acquisition files.
  • EPA Section 608 certification required for HVAC refrigerant handling on files with AC service work.
  • No state income tax improves personal financial modeling on operator-guarantors.
  • Property insurance trajectory. Florida commercial property insurance premium increases affect DSCR on files with meaningful facility cost lines.

Auto Repair Shop SBA mechanics — the short version

SBA 7(a) is the dominant path for auto repair acquisitions, buildouts, equipment, and working capital. Standard 7(a) goes up to $5 million; 7(a) Small Loan streamlines deals under $500K. SBA 504 handles real estate and heavy fixed-asset purchases when the deal includes the property. Minimum 10% equity injection applies; specialist lenders typically want 15-20% on Florida auto repair deals given the higher cost structure. Up to 5% of equity can come from seller financing on full-standby terms.

How does Florida FDACS registration affect SBA auto repair underwriting?
Florida requires motor vehicle repair shops to register with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS) under the Motor Vehicle Repair Act. Registration is straightforward but must be current on acquisition files. Clean registration history with no open consumer complaints underwrites cleanly.
How does hurricane exposure affect Florida auto repair underwriting?
Coastal Florida auto repair shops face hurricane-related revenue disruption and facility damage risk. Specialist Florida lenders factor property-insurance adequacy, hurricane deductible exposure, business-interruption coverage, and post-storm continuity into underwriting. Shops with documented hurricane response plans and adequate insurance underwrite better than those without.

Smaller-tier equipment and working-capital files often close in 45-70 days.
