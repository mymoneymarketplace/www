Texas beauty salons market context

Texas is the second-largest state market for beauty salon SBA lending behind California, and the fastest-growing: 369 loans FY2020-2025 (7.33% national share), $106 million in total approved capital, and $288K average deal size. Deal sizes run 38% above the national beauty salon baseline of $208K, with median loan at $161,000 vs. $80,000 nationally (+101%). YoY volume growth is +124.5%, roughly 14× the national beauty-salon rate of +8.7% -- the highest growth rate of any state-industry combination in the dataset.

Metro distribution: DFW, Houston, Austin, San Antonio

Four major metros dominate Texas beauty salon SBA volume: Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, Austin, and San Antonio. Texas's population growth combined with strong salon industry consolidation drives active acquisition volume. Franchise-brand concepts (Great Clips, Sport Clips, Supercuts, Fantastic Sams) account for a meaningful share of Texas beauty-salon SBA volume alongside independent salon acquisitions.

Charge-off runs 0.79× SBA average — clean-market performance

Texas beauty salon SBA charges off at 1.08% -- a 0.79× ratio versus the SBA cross-industry average of 1.36% and 0.70× the national beauty-salon rate of 1.55%. Texas is meaningfully cleaner than the national beauty-salon average on both baselines. Drivers: population growth expanding customer demand, no-state-income-tax cost advantage for operator-guarantors, moderate operating cost baseline relative to California, and franchise-brand center acquisitions carrying operator-side certainty.

Texas regulatory context for beauty salon SBA underwriting

  • TDLR licensing. Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation licenses cosmetology operators, cosmetology salons, barber shops, and specialty salons (esthetics, manicuring, hair braiding). Salon licensing is separate from individual operator licensing. Acquisition files require verified license transfer or new-owner qualification.
  • Booth rental vs. employer models. Texas allows both booth-rental (independent contractor stylist) and employer (W-2 stylist) salon business models. Lenders model these differently -- booth-rental salons have simpler payroll but higher operator turnover risk; employer salons have more predictable revenue but higher payroll and workers' comp overhead.
  • No state income tax improves personal financial modeling on operator-guarantors.
  • Commercial rent. Texas commercial rent for salon-appropriate locations runs meaningfully below California; DFW and Austin CBD locations approach higher-cost baselines but suburban and secondary markets are substantially cheaper.

Beauty Salon SBA mechanics — the short version

SBA 7(a) is the dominant path for beauty salons acquisitions, buildouts, equipment, and working capital. Standard 7(a) goes up to $5 million; 7(a) Small Loan streamlines deals under $500K. SBA 504 handles real estate and heavy fixed-asset purchases when the deal includes the property. Minimum 10% equity injection applies; specialist lenders typically want 15-20% on Texas beauty salons deals given the higher cost structure. Up to 5% of equity can come from seller financing on full-standby terms.
