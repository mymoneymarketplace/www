Florida landscaping market context

Florida is the second-largest state market for landscaping SBA lending: 417 loans FY2020-2025 (6.99% national share), $196 million in total approved capital, and $471K average deal size. Deal sizes run 55% above the national landscaping baseline of $303K, with median loan at $222,300 vs. $100,000 nationally (+122%). YoY volume growth is -1.15%, better than the national landscaping rate of -4.23% but still contracting.

Metro distribution

Four Florida metros drive landscaping SBA volume: Miami / Fort Lauderdale, Orlando, Tampa Bay, and Jacksonville. Commercial landscaping demand is strong across Florida (HOAs, resorts, office parks, hospitals, hospitality corridors) but the residential and small-commercial tier carries higher operational volatility. Franchise-brand acquisition options (U.S. Lawns, The Grounds Guys, Weed Man) account for a modest share of Florida landscaping SBA volume.

Honest framing: charge-off runs 2.82× SBA average — the highest landscaping state combination

Florida landscaping SBA charges off at 3.84% -- a 2.82× ratio versus the SBA cross-industry average of 1.36% and 3.15× the national landscaping rate of 1.22%. This is among the highest state-industry charge-off ratios in the entire SBA FY2020-2025 dataset. Drivers of the elevated performance:

  • Hurricane exposure on receivables and equipment. Florida landscaping companies face hurricane-related revenue disruption, delayed recovery-related project payments, and equipment damage risk. Post-storm working-capital gaps stress DSCR meaningfully.
  • Fuel and equipment cost sensitivity. Landscaping is a fuel- and equipment-intensive category. Fuel price volatility compresses margins on files with meaningful drive-time (Florida's spread-out metros) and heavy equipment operation.
  • Labor concentration risk. Florida landscaping labor is highly concentrated in H-2B visa and undocumented labor pools. Labor supply disruption (immigration policy shifts, workforce shortages, wage inflation) compresses landscaping margins disproportionately in Florida.
  • Rapid-growth-market instability. Florida's rapid population growth has drawn many operators into landscaping ownership without established commercial contract books or fleet management experience. Files with less-established books charge off at particularly elevated rates.
  • Seasonality (marginal in Florida) is offset by hurricane season disruption. Year-round Florida landscaping demand doesn't smooth performance as much as it might because hurricane-season disruption cycles create their own working-capital gaps.

None of this means Florida landscaping SBA is unfundable. Files that succeed do so with established commercial-contract-heavy books (HOA, resort, or municipal contracts with long-term stability), fleet-management discipline, disciplined operators with clean insurance history, and lenders who model Florida-specific structural risks explicitly rather than applying national landscaping benchmarks. Specialist Florida landscaping SBA lenders factor these risks into underwriting from day one.

Florida regulatory context for landscaping SBA underwriting

  • Florida DACS licensing (limited). Florida requires Commercial Fertilizer Applicator certification for commercial fertilizer application, and Pest Control Business licensing for lawn pest and disease treatment. Landscape architecture requires separate licensing. Clean licensing history underwrites cleanly.
  • H-2B visa program participation. Many Florida landscaping companies rely on H-2B seasonal labor. Files that document H-2B compliance and worker acquisition plans underwrite better than those that don't.
  • No state income tax improves personal financial modeling on operator-guarantors.
  • Fleet and equipment financing. SBA 7(a) commonly used for equipment upgrades, truck fleets, and mower/blower/trimmer equipment on landscaping files.

Landscaping Company SBA mechanics — the short version

SBA 7(a) is the dominant path for landscaping acquisitions, buildouts, equipment, and working capital. Standard 7(a) goes up to $5 million; 7(a) Small Loan streamlines deals under $500K. SBA 504 handles real estate and heavy fixed-asset purchases when the deal includes the property. Minimum 10% equity injection applies; specialist lenders typically want 15-20% on Florida landscaping deals given the higher cost structure. Up to 5% of equity can come from seller financing on full-standby terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get an SBA loan for a landscaping company in Florida?
Yes -- but honest framing first. Florida is the second-largest single-state landscaping SBA market (417 loans FY2020-2025, 7% of national volume, $196 million in total approved capital) but the charge-off rate is one of the highest in the SBA dataset at 3.84% (2.82x the SBA cross-industry average). Standard SBA 7(a) minimum 10% equity injection applies; specialist Florida landscaping lenders typically want 15-25% given the elevated performance history.
Why does Florida landscaping SBA charge off so much higher than the average?
Florida landscaping SBA charges off at 3.84% -- 2.82x the SBA cross-industry average and 3.15x the national landscaping rate. This is among the highest state-industry charge-off ratios in the FY2020-2025 SBA dataset. Drivers include hurricane exposure on receivables and equipment, fuel and equipment cost sensitivity, labor concentration risk (H-2B visa program, workforce disruption), and rapid-growth-market instability (newer operators without established commercial-contract books). Files with established commercial-contract books still underwrite favorably.
What kind of Florida landscaping file actually underwrites cleanly?
Files with commercial-contract-heavy books (HOA, resort, municipal contracts with long-term stability), fleet-management discipline, clean insurance history, hurricane-preparedness plans, and documented H-2B or workforce acquisition plans underwrite meaningfully better than residential-heavy or newer-operator files. Specialist Florida landscaping lenders factor these structural risks into underwriting from day one rather than applying national benchmarks.
What's the typical SBA landscaping loan size in Florida?
Average Florida landscaping SBA loan is approximately $471,000 -- 55% above the national landscaping baseline of $303K. Median is $222,300 vs. $100,000 nationally (+122%). Deal sizes reflect Florida's mix of larger commercial-contract-heavy landscaping acquisitions and mid-sized residential-heavy files, with fleet and equipment often a meaningful component.

How does hurricane exposure affect Florida landscaping underwriting?
Florida landscaping companies face hurricane-related revenue disruption, delayed recovery-related project payments, and equipment damage risk. Specialist Florida lenders factor property and equipment insurance adequacy, business-interruption coverage, hurricane deductible exposure, and post-storm continuity into underwriting. Companies with documented hurricane response plans and adequate insurance underwrite better than those without.
How long does an SBA loan take to close for a Florida landscaping company?
60-90 days is typical for a Florida landscaping acquisition with TD Bank, Huntington, Newtek, or another specialist lender. Files require verified Florida DACS Commercial Fertilizer Applicator certification and Pest Control Business licensing where applicable. Smaller-tier equipment and working-capital files often close in 45-70 days. Given Florida's elevated charge-off history, extra due-diligence steps often add days to the process.
