Florida insurance agencies market context

Florida is the second-largest state market for insurance agency SBA lending: 437 loans FY2020-2025 (10.28% national share), $267 million in total approved capital, and $611K average deal size. Deal sizes run 28% above the national insurance agency baseline of $478K, with median loan at $285,000 vs. $225,000 nationally (+27%). YoY volume growth is +17.4%, close to the national insurance agency rate of +20.5%.

Metro distribution: Miami, Orlando, Tampa, Jacksonville

Four Florida metros drive insurance agency SBA volume: Miami / Fort Lauderdale, Orlando, Tampa Bay, and Jacksonville. Florida's demographic mix (retirees, property owners with hurricane exposure, high boat and RV ownership) creates unusually strong demand for property, marine, and specialty insurance. Aging independent agency principals selling to younger operators drives active acquisition volume across the state.

Charge-off runs 0.34× SBA average — clean-market performance

Florida insurance agency SBA charges off at 0.46% -- a 0.34× ratio versus the SBA cross-industry average of 1.36% and 0.43× the national insurance agency rate of 1.06%. Florida is meaningfully cleaner than the national insurance agency average on both baselines. Drivers: insurance agency SBA at the national level performs cleanly because agencies acquire established books of business with recurring commission revenue, and Florida amplifies this with strong demand across property, auto, health, and specialty lines. The asset-light structure (no inventory, minimal capex, book-of-business revenue) fits SBA 7(a) underwriting well.

Florida regulatory context for insurance agency SBA underwriting

  • Florida Department of Financial Services licensing. Florida requires insurance producer licensing (Property & Casualty, Life & Health, or specialty lines). Acquisition files require verified license transfer or new-owner qualification. Clean licensing history and no open regulatory matters underwrite cleanly.
  • Property insurance market complexity. Florida's hard property insurance market (double-digit annual premium increases, carriers exiting the market) has actually driven demand for agency services and E&O differentiation. Agencies specializing in Florida coastal property insurance or specialty lines have strong economics.
  • No state income tax improves personal financial modeling on agency-owner-guarantors.
  • Book-of-business valuation. Independent agency acquisitions are typically valued at 2.5-4x annual commissions depending on line mix, carrier relationships, and retention rates. Specialist agency SBA lenders understand these multiples and structure equity injection accordingly.

Insurance Agency SBA mechanics — the short version

SBA 7(a) is the dominant path for insurance agencies acquisitions, buildouts, equipment, and working capital. Standard 7(a) goes up to $5 million; 7(a) Small Loan streamlines deals under $500K. SBA 504 handles real estate and heavy fixed-asset purchases when the deal includes the property. Minimum 10% equity injection applies; specialist lenders typically want 15-20% on Florida insurance agencies deals given the higher cost structure. Up to 5% of equity can come from seller financing on full-standby terms.

How does book-of-business valuation affect SBA underwriting?
Independent agency acquisitions are typically valued at 2.5-4x annual commissions depending on line mix, carrier relationships, and retention rates. Higher-quality books (property with sticky renewals, life insurance with long-tail commissions) command higher multiples. Specialist agency SBA lenders understand these multiples and structure equity injection accordingly. Files with detailed carrier-by-carrier commission history underwrite cleanly.
How does Florida's property insurance market affect agencies?
Florida's hard property insurance market has driven demand for agency services and E&O differentiation. Agencies specializing in Florida coastal property insurance, wind coverage, flood, or specialty lines have strong economics. Agencies with concentrated exposure to any single struggling carrier face different risk than those with diversified appointments. Specialist Florida agency SBA lenders factor carrier diversification into underwriting.
How long does an SBA loan take to close for a Florida insurance agency?
60-90 days is typical for a Florida insurance agency acquisition with TD Bank, Readycap, BayFirst, or another specialist lender. Standalone book-of-business purchases (without the entity) often close in 45-75 days given the simpler asset structure. Startup and new-agency files typically run 75-100 days given the extra operator-profile underwriting.
